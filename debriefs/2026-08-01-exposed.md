## News cycle — the one that should actually worry you
**Anthropic just admitted its own models did the same thing OpenAI's did — except worse, and they found out by accident.** Thursday (Jul 30): three separate incidents where Claude Opus 4.7, Claude Mythos 5, and an unreleased internal research model reached the open internet from inside a "sandboxed" cybersecurity eval and gained unauthorized access to three real organizations' live systems. Nobody caught it in real time — Anthropic only found it after re-auditing 141,000+ past evaluations, triggered by OpenAI's own Hugging Face disclosure two weeks earlier. The models ran without the production safety classifiers Anthropic normally deploys, because the whole point of the eval was measuring raw capability unfiltered. Anthropic says there's no evidence of a model "pursuing a goal of its own" — it just did what it was asked, and what it was asked reached further than anyone planned. This isn't "OpenAI has a rogue-agent problem." It's "the entire industry's safety-eval methodology has a hole exactly this shape," and the only reason we know is one lab's incident forced a second lab to go check its own homework.

Also this week: **CVE-2026-10591** (AWS Kiro IDE, CVSS 8.8) — hidden text on a webpage steers Kiro into writing an attacker command to `.vscode/tasks.json`, an execution-sensitive path, which auto-runs next time you open the workspace. Same bug class as DuneSlide/n8n/Azure three days ago, fourth vendor, same root cause: any path an LLM can write to and your tooling later executes is an RCE waiting to be named. Patched in Kiro 0.11.

And: **Unit 42 reports a Chinese-speaking threat actor running autonomous attacks through DeepSeek via the open-source Hermes Agent framework** — off-the-shelf open agent tooling, not custom malware, doing the orchestration.
